Sewer Scope Inspection in Denver: A Home Buyer's Checklist

A standard home inspection is visual and stops at the surface. The buried sewer lateral from the house to the public main is not part of it, and on an older Denver house that is a real gap.

1. Decide whether the house warrants one

Anything built before about 1980 is a strong candidate, and especially anything from roughly 1945 to 1972, when some Denver homes were connected with Orangeburg fibre pipe. A lateral that has never been documented is worth scoping at any age. A rebuilt house can still sit on an original lateral, so a new kitchen tells you nothing about the pipe to the street.

2. Book it early in the contingency window

A scope is only useful while there is still room to negotiate or walk away. Tell whoever you call the contract deadline at the start, not after. Sewer scope inspection in Denver is scheduled around that date, and if it cannot be fitted in time to be useful you should be told immediately.

3. Sort out access before the appointment

The camera usually goes in through a cleanout: an exterior capped fitting near the foundation, or one on the stack in the basement. Ask the listing side where it is. On many older Denver properties nobody knows, and if there is no cleanout, a toilet may need to be pulled. Finding that out on the day costs half the window.

4. Know what the footage can show

Root intrusion at clay joints, offset or separated joints, bellies that hold standing water, scale narrowing old cast iron, cracks, deformed Orangeburg and collapsed sections, plus the pipe material and where one material changes to another. The rod is marked, so every finding comes with a distance from the access point, and a surface locator can mark position and approximate depth.

5. Read findings in proportion

Ask how much of the line is affected, what it is made of, and whether the surrounding pipe is sound. One root joint is a cleaning conversation; twelve is a replacement conversation. A shallow belly is common and often liveable. And plenty of scopes find a sound line and stop there, which is exactly what you were paying to learn.

6. Ask about the report

Ask in advance what you will receive and in what form, so you know what you can pass to your agent or the seller.
